## 1. One-line concept

**This world is a book that is fading, and you are the only page that was never written.**

## 2. Design intent: the world is the mechanics made flesh

The first principle of this setting is that every framework mechanic has an in-world embodiment — every rule the player learns is a natural consequence of the world:

| Framework mechanic | In-world embodiment |
|---|---|
| D20 check | **The Fatedie**: the ritual by which the Unwritten wrest the right to rewrite from the Weave |
| World State Board / flags | **The text of the Weave**: facts that have already been written |
| Branching directed graph | Forks of fate: the path that was written vs the path the Unwritten walk |
| Doom Clock | **Progress of the Fading**: how much of the world is being erased |
| History Scroll (screen ⑧) | The Unwritten's **private codex**: the event stream you wrote with your own hand |
| Reputation | How each faction regards "the one who was never written" |
| Content-pack expansion | **Fragments**: pages scattered across the continent, waiting to be recovered and rewritten |

## 3. World overview

### 3.1 The continent: Elowen

A continent that was *written* into being. Mountains, rivers, city-states, every person's life — all are fixed text on the Scroll of All Things. At birth each person receives their own page of the codex, recording their name, trade, marriage and the day of their death. Most people live peacefully under the protection of the ink: **to be written is to exist.**

### 3.2 Chronicle of ages (summary)

| Age | Name | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| First | Age of First Ink | The Weavers descend and set down the first stroke; the world takes shape out of nothing |
| Second | Age of Scribing | All things are written one by one; order is established |
| Third | Age of War | The nations make war — later it is discovered that even the wars were on the Scroll |
| Fourth | Age of Gold Ink | The Scriptorium is founded; the order of scribing reaches its height |
| Fifth | Age of the Torn Scroll | The Uninked rebellion; the first "torn page" appears |
| Sixth | **Age of the Codex (current)** | The Fading arrives; text begins to vanish; the Unwritten are born one after another |

## 4. Core setting

### 4.1 The Weavers and the Scroll of All Things

The Weavers are the divine race that wrote the world. They have long been silent — some say they laid down their pens, others that their pens were stolen. The Scroll of All Things they left behind keeps writing itself, sustaining the fixed fate of all things.

### 4.2 The Fading

The calamity of the Sixth Age: the ink on the Scroll is fading. When a village's text disappears, the village — and everyone's memory of it — evaporates, as if it had never existed. The Fading began at the edges of the continent and is spreading toward the heartland. **No one knows why the ink is fading; this is the central mystery of the first Epic.**

### 4.3 The Unwritten

Anomalies that appeared together with the Fading: people born without a codex page, their fate entirely blank.

- Their choices are not predetermined — **at every key decision they must roll the Fatedie to win from the Weave the right to rewrite reality**;
- The Scriptorium sees them as living typos, the Uninked as proof of freedom, the Salvage Guild as walking business opportunities;
- The player's party is 1 player character + up to 3 companions. Each companion has "a past that was written" and "a page they want to rewrite" (the narrative root of the Bond system).

### 4.4 The Fatedie

A twenty-sided bone die left behind by the Weavers; only the Unwritten can make it sound. The moment the die settles, the Weave leaves the roller a single instant of blank space — **in that instant, reality can be rewritten**. This explains why: checks must be rolled; key checks get a full-screen presentation (the moment fate is rewritten); and why payment may never touch the dice (not even the gods may).

### 4.5 Inkbound ↔ Blankward (the alignment axis)

How the framework's Alignment slider is expressed in this world:

- **Inkbound**: believes the text *is* order; inclined to repair the Scroll and uphold what is written (the Scriptorium's path);
- **Blankward**: believes blank space *is* freedom; inclined to tear up the predetermined and let fate choose itself (the Uninked's path);
- Alignment shapes the ending type: mend this book, burn it, or — write a new one.

## 5. Endgame threat and the Doom Clock

- **Epic-level Doom Clock = Fading level**: 0–10 segments, rising and falling with campaign progress and key choices;
- Each region has a local Fading clock; when it fills, a regional event fires (a town vanishes, an NPC is forgotten, a map entrance closes permanently — time edges made narrative);
- The Fading is not a pure countdown: some choices **accelerate** it (the cost of the Uninked route), some **delay** it (the reward of the Inkbound route), and extreme choices can even locally "re-ink" the world.

## 6. Factions (starting reputation)

| Faction | Stance | Toward the Unwritten | Reputation reward direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Scriptorium | Upholds the order of the Scroll, repairs the Fading | Wary exploitation | Supplies, intelligence, safe zones |
| The Uninked | Tears up predetermined fate | Fanatical devotion | Hidden passages, forbidden arts |
| The Salvage Guild | Recovers scattered fragments for profit | Pure business | Gear, Compendium entries, appraisal |
| Free City of Ashenport (launch-region faction) | Citizens surviving on the faded edge | Fear and hope in equal measure | Manpower, strongholds, popular support |

## 7. Geography: chapter regions

The continent is divided into "chapters", each sized to the content of one campaign book:

- **Ashenport**: the launch campaign region. The port city the Fading reached first; half its residents have already been "revoked", and those left live with half their memories;
- **The Holy City of Scribes**: headquarters of the Scriptorium, beneath its gold-ink dome (DLC hook);
- **The Torn-Page Wastes**: a lawless land ripped open by the Uninked, where text is broken and reality is disordered (sub-game hook: disordered-rules encounters);
- **The Deep Ink Archive**: where the Scroll of All Things is kept; the stage for the Epic's finale (long-term content).

## 8. Narrative hooks for the two campaign forms

The campaign-mode declaration (framework §7) has an in-world identity, so forced modes "have a reason":

- **Landscape · Spatial campaigns** = "chapters written in plain text": battlefields, city defenses, great battles — space is clearly written;
- **Portrait · Temporal campaigns** = "fragments and remembrance": regions eaten by the Fading are only fragmentary scenes that can only be replayed and advanced one room at a time — **a forced-portrait campaign is, narratively, "this stretch of history is already in ruins"**.

## 8.1 Who the hero can be: classes and backgrounds (P6.2)

The three classes are three ways Ashenport survives the Fading:

- **Harbor Guard** — the city's last uniformed order, keeping the piers when the names on them fade. Shield and stubbornness; they stand where the ink-tide comes ashore.
- **Scavenger** — the mudlarks and wreck-pickers of the Salvage Guild's margins. Quick hands, a hook knife, smoke and a nose for what the tide gives up.
- **Ink Adept** — a scribe who learned that written ink can still answer. They write wounds closed and bolts into being, at the cost of a little of themselves.

Backgrounds bind the hero to a faction before the first page: a **Dockhand** grew up under Old Fei's crane; a **Scriptorium Novice** copied pages in the archive until the pages began to empty; an **Uninked Runaway** fled the ink tithe and owes the Uninked a debt; a **Salvager's Apprentice** knows the price of every fragment. Each grants a keepsake item, a starting reputation and a written backstory, and opens extra lines at matching scenes (doc 11 §1.1).

## 8.2 Companions: canon sheets

Every companion has "a past that was written" and "a page they want to rewrite" (§4.3). This section is the **canon registry**. A writer may fill an *open* item once, in a quest, and must then record it here in the same change (quest id + one line), so the next quest doesn't contradict it. *Locked* items are already established on screen and must not be contradicted.

### Kelan (`companion_kelan`)

- **Locked:**
  - She is at the Unwritten's side from the first night of ink-rain (N1-7) and stays in the party.
  - She reads old bindings and dates records at a glance (N1-1ma, "Kelan dates the ledgers"), and leads History checks in the archive thread (campaign book §4).
  - She heals with written ink (Kelan's Patch) and fights with an ink bolt.
  - Stats: d6 Hit Die; proficient in history and soothe.
  - She smooths the paper for the hero's new line in ending E3.
- **Temperament (locked by her lines):** quiet, precise, protective. She asks before she acts.
- **Open:**
  - Her written past: who taught her to read bindings, and where.
  - The page she wants to rewrite.
  - How she relates to the Scriptorium.

### Old Fei (`companion_laofei`)

- **Locked:**
  - He is the harbour crane operator; Dockhands grew up under his crane, and he recognises a Dockhand's tally.
  - He handles salvage practically (he dries readable pages on an oilcloth, N1-1mb).
  - He fights with a boathook and braces to protect the party.
  - Stats: d8 Hit Die; proficient in athletics and perception.
- **Temperament:** dry, practical, unsentimental in words and sentimental in deeds.
- **Filled by arc_crane (story/05):** for thirty years he chalked every crew he worked with into one ledger, kept in the tally house by the breakwater; the Fading began eating it from the newest crews back. Crews he names: "the lad who lost two fingers to the chain", "Big Hu". If the player kept the ledger for him, he gave away his old crane token.
- **Open:** the page he wants to rewrite, and his family.

## 9. Compendium Lore entry framework

Lore entries in the Compendium (screen ⑥) are organised into four series; completing a series unlocks hidden content:

| Series | Content | Completion reward direction |
|---|---|---|
| Weaver's Fragments | Shards of the gods' record of writing the world | Hidden storyline revealing the truth of the Fading |
| Register of the Revoked | Codex scraps of the people and places erased by the Fading | Unlocks a "Remembrance" campaign (forced portrait) |
| Treatise on the Fatedie | Legends of past Unwritten | Dice cosmetics (pure presentation item slot) |
| Ashenport Gazetteer | Local chronicle of the launch region | Ashenport reputation and shop discounts |

## 10. Hook for the first main campaign (seed of M1)

You wake to the sound of the tide in Ashenport, and there is no codex page in your arms — you are one of the Unwritten. The city is fading: first the name of the harbor, then a street, then someone's daughter. All three factions come looking for you at once, because the Fatedie in your hand is the last pen this city has.

— The storyline node graph of the campaign book *Ashenport Fragments* is designed separately on top of this world foundation (`../framework/03-TASKS.md` P1.2).
